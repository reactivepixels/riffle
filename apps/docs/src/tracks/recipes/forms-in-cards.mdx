A card is not required to be read-only content. If it holds a real form field, two pieces of engine
behaviour, both already covered in [Accessibility](/riffle/_track_/guides/accessibility/), are what
make that work without any extra configuration on your part.

## Typing does not move the stack

Riffle checks the keydown's own target before mapping arrow keys, `Home` or `End` to a stack move
(see [Accessibility](/riffle/_track_/guides/accessibility/#what-riffle-does)). An `<input>`,
`<textarea>`, `<select>`, or a `contenteditable` element is left alone entirely: the keydown reaches
the field itself, so the caret moves and characters get typed exactly as they would outside a stack.
There is nothing to opt into.

## A background card's field is unreachable, not just visually behind

Every card except the active one is `inert` (see [Accessibility](/riffle/_track_/guides/accessibility/)).
An `<input>` inside a background card cannot receive focus: not by Tab, not by a screen reader's
virtual cursor, not by a direct `.focus()` call from your own code. Advance the stack in the live demo
above, then Tab through the page: only the front card's field is ever reachable, and the note you
typed on a card you have since navigated away from stays exactly where you left it, simply out of
reach until that card is active again.

## Where focus goes when the active card changes

If a field inside the active card is focused and the stack moves to a new card, focus follows, exactly
as [Accessibility](/riffle/_track_/guides/accessibility/#what-riffle-does) describes for any card's
content: it lands on the **new active card's own root element**, never on a field inside it, and never
the specific field you happened to be in before.

If you want focus to land inside the new card's first field instead, put it there yourself, in your
own change handler: the engine updates the new card's accessibility state, including its own
focus-follow onto that card's root, before it fires its own change event, so your own redirect runs
after the engine's and is what sticks.

## Each card's own field

<Snippet name="card-fields" />

A few things worth calling out:

<Only fw="vanilla">

- **Each input's `id` is built from the film's own index** (`forms-in-cards-note-${index}`), so
  `<label for>` genuinely targets the right field even though every card's markup is assembled the
  same way.

</Only>

<Only fw="react">

- **Each input's `id` is built from `useId()` plus the card's index**, so `<label htmlFor>` genuinely
  targets the right field even though every card renders the same JSX. A hardcoded `id` would collide
  across cards, and across multiple stacks on one page.

</Only>

<Only fw="vue">

- **Each input's `id` is built from a per-instance prefix plus the card's index** (Vue has no `useId`
  equivalent), so `<label for>` genuinely targets the right field even though every card's template
  is the same.

</Only>

- **The label is real markup, not a placeholder standing in for one.** A `placeholder` disappears the
  moment you type and most screen readers do not treat it as a substitute for a label; every version
  of this recipe uses both, a real `<label>` for the accessible name and a `placeholder` for the
  example prompt text.
- **Nothing here is Riffle-specific.** The stack does not know or care that its cards contain form
  fields; every behaviour on this page falls out of the roving-tabindex and `inert` mechanics that
  apply to any element inside a card, form field or not.
